# Curriculum Design

## Principles

- Assessment comes before placement.
- Progress means performing a communicative can-do task, not seeing a word list.
- Retrieval and spaced practice precede new material.
- Each short session focuses on due items and at most a few new concepts.
- Romaji and English remain visible throughout beginner conversation.

The phase sequence follows CEFR/JF-style can-do progression: survival interaction, familiar identity statements, everyday actions, simple plans, connected daily contexts, short conversation loops, and later literacy support.

## Literacy timing

- Kana begins only after the learner can sustain basic familiar exchanges and opts into the bridge.
- Stories begin after enough grammar and vocabulary can be recycled in four-to-eight sentences with few new concepts.
- Kanji is meaning-first and limited to already-known spoken/kana words after kana is stable.

## Why progression is constrained

Uncontrolled novelty produces shallow familiarity and a growing review burden. The planner therefore prioritizes prerequisite gaps, due items, repeated mistakes, and can-do mastery. Side questions are preserved, and advanced accidental concepts are deferred rather than lost or forced into daily review.
